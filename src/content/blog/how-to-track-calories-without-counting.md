Counting calories sounds simple in theory: eat less than you burn, lose weight. But in practice, manually logging every bite is exhausting, time-consuming, and often leads to an unhealthy relationship with food.

The good news? You can still hit your nutrition goals without turning every meal into a math problem.

## Why Traditional Calorie Counting Fails Most People

Research consistently shows that manual calorie tracking is inaccurate by 20–50%. People underestimate portion sizes, forget to log snacks, and eventually give up entirely. The cognitive load is simply too high.

More importantly, obsessing over numbers can trigger anxiety and disordered eating patterns in some people.

### 2. Focus on Protein and Vegetables

Instead of counting every calorie, track just two things: protein intake and vegetable servings. When you hit 25–30g of protein per meal and eat 2+ portions of vegetables, your total calories naturally fall into a healthy range.

### 3. Follow a Pre-Built Meal Plan

You just cook and eat. The math is done for you.

### 4. Use the Hand Portion Method

When you cannot weigh food or take a photo:
- **Protein** = palm size
- **Carbs** = cupped hand
- **Fats** = thumb
- **Vegetables** = two fists

This approach is backed by research and surprisingly accurate for most people.

### 5. Weekly Average Over Daily Perfection

Stop trying to be perfect every single day. Track your nutrition on most days and check your weekly average. One "bad" day does not ruin progress if the weekly trend is positive.
